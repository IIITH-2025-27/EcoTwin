\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

EcoTwin is an end-to-end geospatial ecosystem analog discovery
platform designed around the question: \emph{which ecosystems
elsewhere exhibit similar spatial structure, and what can their
historical trajectories tell us about a target ecosystem?} HydroLAKES
polygons provide ecosystem boundaries; a buffered, tiled Sentinel-2
acquisition pipeline through Google Earth Engine yields
one-kilometre analysis cells, converted into six-band Prithvi
embeddings, coverage-pooled to one 768-dimensional lake-year vector
and indexed in PostgreSQL/pgvector. A query ecosystem is compared as
a \emph{five-year trajectory} rather than a single snapshot, sliding
across each candidate's history to find its best-matching window,
and the ranked results are exposed through an interactive map and
profile views with historical index trajectories.

The deployed system holds a complete, fully processed cohort ---
\textbf{829 active lakes across 26 Indian states}, ten consecutive
years (2016--2025), \textbf{8{,}227 lake-year embeddings},
\textbf{19{,}046} imagery tiles, and \textbf{233{,}002} analysis
cells, with no outstanding failures at any pipeline stage. The
forecasting layer is analog-based and non-parametric: rather than
decoding an embedding back into reflectance, it uses the embedding
space only to \emph{rank} analogs, then transfers their measured
year-on-year changes onto the target as a rank-weighted directional
forecast, traceable to the specific lakes, years, and deltas that
produced it. The system is demonstrated on five Ramsar-designated
wetlands --- Chilika, Wular, Loktak, Tso Morari, and Vembanad ---
reported in full in Chapter~\ref{chap:ramsar}.

\textbf{Keywords:}
Geospatial Foundation Models, Prithvi, Remote Sensing, Sentinel-2,
HydroLAKES, Ecosystem Analogs, Vector Search, pgvector, NDCI,
Red-Edge Indices, Ramsar Wetlands, Trajectory Similarity

\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chap:introduction}

\section{Background and Problem Statement}

Earth observation satellites provide repeated measurements of
ecosystems at spatial scales that cannot be obtained economically
through field observations alone, and Sentinel-2's multispectral
optical imagery is well suited to vegetation, water, land-cover, and
surface-condition analysis. Rather than beginning with a specific
question such as vegetation change or water extent, EcoTwin treats
ecosystem comparison as a retrieval problem: satellite observations
are converted into learned feature vectors and indexed, so a lake can
be compared against a large reference collection without repeatedly
processing raw imagery, asking whether another ecosystem looks
similar in its spatial-spectral representation.

Several engineering constraints shape how this is actually solved.
Lake boundaries are irregular polygons rather than fixed-size image
squares, and large satellite scenes are expensive to download and
store, so large ecosystems may require multiple image tiles rather
than a single export. Historical Sentinel-2 collections do not
expose identical quality bands across all years, and cloud or
invalid-pixel contamination must be controlled for regardless of
collection; every resulting embedding must remain associated with
its source geometry and lake/year metadata rather than existing as
an anonymous vector, and online similarity search must be
substantially cheaper than recomputing embeddings from scratch.

\section{Core EcoTwin Hypothesis}

EcoTwin is based on the operational hypothesis:

\begin{quote}
\emph{Ecosystems elsewhere that are structurally similar in
geospatial feature space can provide useful analogs for understanding
the spatial and historical behaviour of a target ecosystem.}
\end{quote}

The hypothesis separates two tasks. The first is \textbf{analog
discovery}: finding ecosystems that resemble the target in the
learned spatial-spectral representation. The second is
\textbf{trajectory analysis}: comparing the historical environmental
indicators of the target against those of its discovered analogs.

\section{Project Evolution and Contributions}

The implementation evolved from an initial design of standardized
$5\times5$ km ecosystem blocks toward a lake-centred buffered
imagery stage, local raster merging, and $1\times1$ km analysis
cells: the larger imagery tiles are an I/O and Earth Engine
management mechanism, while the smaller cells are the units from
which embeddings are generated, which lets large lakes be processed
without forcing the foundation model to operate directly on
irregular polygons.

Two further pivots shaped the final system. First,
\textbf{snapshot similarity gave way to trajectory similarity}:
comparing single query and candidate embeddings proved too weak a
notion of ecosystem resemblance, since two lakes can coincide in one
year and diverge over a decade, so the implemented system instead
compares a \emph{five-year trajectory} of the query against every
five-year sliding window in each candidate's history, scoring by the
best-matching window and retaining its position in time
(Section~\ref{sec:trajectory}). Second, an \textbf{embedding decoder
was designed and then abandoned}: the original design intended to
decode a predicted future embedding back into spectral indices,
which would have required training an inversion of Prithvi's
768-dimensional space --- not calibrated, and exactly the kind of
opacity the analog architecture exists to avoid. The implemented
layer instead uses embeddings \emph{only} to rank analogs, and
computes predicted quantities from the analogs' measured band
statistics already in the database
(Section~\ref{sec:forecast_method}): a transfer of observed change,
not a reconstruction of an imagined image.

The project's contribution is the resulting end-to-end
lake-to-embedding-to-analog pipeline, executed to completion over an
829-lake, ten-year cohort with no residual failures: a satellite-image
acquisition strategy built on Google Earth Engine and local raster
processing with polygon-aware spatial subdivision and coverage
filtering; Prithvi-based six-band representations with
coverage-weighted pooling from cell to lake level, persisted as
768-dimensional vectors and retrieved through trajectory-based
analog search; the decoder-free forecasting method above; a
band-honest ecological index set computed only from channels the
imagery actually contains; and a FastAPI/React-Leaflet application
with automated PDF reporting, evaluated on five Ramsar wetlands of
international importance.

\section{Overview}

EcoTwin combines static geographic information (HydroLAKES polygons
and metadata) with repeated satellite observations (Sentinel-2), so
that the static layer defines \emph{where} an ecosystem is while the
satellite layer defines \emph{what it looks like through time}. The
database representation retains the identifying, geographic, and
descriptive fields it needs downstream --- lake identifier and name,
country, polygon geometry, area, elevation, pour-point coordinates,
lake type, average depth, total volume, and watershed area ---
summarised with their pipeline role in Table~\ref{tab:hydrolakes_fields}.
The complete HydroLAKES-derived inventory is much larger than the
processing cohort: the pipeline was narrowed to a selected subset of
lakes so that multi-year imagery and embeddings could be generated
within available storage, Earth Engine, and compute budgets.

\section{Sentinel-2 and Temporal Coverage}

Sentinel-2 multispectral imagery, filtered, composited, and exported
through Google Earth Engine, is the primary remote-sensing input. The
principal collection is \code{COPERNICUS/S2\_SR\_HARMONIZED}, with a
fallback to \code{COPERNICUS/S2} where the surface-reflectance
harmonized collection provides no observations --- particularly
important for early Sentinel-2 years.

Ingestion was extended from an initially planned 2018--2025 window
back to 2016, giving ten consecutive years. Table~\ref{tab:year_coverage}
shows the result: from 2018 onward every one of the 829 active lakes
has a complete, embedded, feature-extracted record, while 2016 and
2017 fall slightly short because Sentinel-2B did not launch until
March 2017, leaving a single-satellite, longer-revisit period in
which some lakes lacked enough cloud-free acquisitions for a usable
annual composite.

The embedding input uses six bands, \code{B2--B7} (blue, green, red,
narrow near-infrared, and two short-wave infrared channels),
selected consistently from the exported GeoTIFFs before model
inference.

\section{Environmental Indices: A Band-Honest Index Set}

The merged lake imagery contains only \code{B2--B7} --- not B8
(broad NIR), B11, or B12 (SWIR) --- so the textbook forms of NDVI,
NDWI, and NBR, which all require B8 or SWIR, cannot be computed.
Computing them from substituted bands while still calling them
NDVI, NDWI, or NBR would misreport what was measured, so EcoTwin
instead uses five indices chosen to be fully supported by the bands
actually present and named for what they really are, computed from
the per-band \emph{mean} reflectance of each lake-year over pixels
inside the lake polygon: NDCI ($\frac{B5-B4}{B5+B4}$), the primary
eutrophication/algal-bloom indicator, since B5 (705\,nm, red edge)
is where chlorophyll-a reflectance rises sharply while B4 (665\,nm)
remains absorbed; NDVI-B7 ($\frac{B7-B4}{B7+B4}$), substituting the
absent B8 with the narrow-NIR red-edge channel B7 (783\,nm) for a
vegetation-vigor measure that tracks floating and emergent
vegetation --- macrophytes, water hyacinth, and the \emph{phumdi}
mats of some Indian wetlands --- with the substitution recorded in
the name rather than concealed; NDWI ($\frac{B3-B7}{B3+B7}$), the
same substitution applied to the water index, which falls as
vegetation raises B7 and so acts as NDVI-B7's natural mirror signal;
Turbidity ($\frac{B4-B3}{B4+B3}$), which rises with suspended
sediment; and Red-Edge Slope ($\frac{B7-B5}{78\text{nm}}$), the
gradient across the red-edge region, sensitive to pigment
concentration and canopy density independently of the
normalized-difference family.

B8 is deliberately excluded from the Prithvi input, whose band count
and normalization constants are fixed by the model's training
configuration, but is useful as an independent descriptor, so it is
acquired separately (Section~\ref{sec:b8}) and stored alongside the
other band statistics for every lake-year.

A valid ecosystem record requires a valid lake polygon and year, a
successful imagery query and usable composite, valid exported raster
data, valid spatial cells after coverage filtering, and a
successfully generated embedding for each retained cell; the
pipeline distinguishes successful, failed, skipped, and
already-completed work so that long-running jobs can be resumed.

\chapter{Detailed EcoTwin Methodology}
\label{chap:methodology}

\section{End-to-End Pipeline}

The implemented workflow is:

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Lake\ Inventory
&\rightarrow Boundary\ Validation\\
&\rightarrow Buffering
\rightarrow Imagery\ Tiling\\
&\rightarrow Sentinel\text{-}2\ Composite
\rightarrow GeoTIFF\\
&\rightarrow Local\ Mosaic
\rightarrow 1\,km\ Cells\\
&\rightarrow Coverage\ Filter
\rightarrow Prithvi\\
&\rightarrow 768D\ Embeddings
\rightarrow pgvector\\
&\rightarrow Cosine\ Search
\rightarrow Analogs\\
&\rightarrow Temporal\ Profiles
\rightarrow Forecast
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Every stage is \textbf{idempotent at the row level}: each writes a

\code{failed}) before doing any work, and a re-run skips rows
already \code{completed} rather than reprocessing them. Stage 7
(B8) has no code dependency on stages 4--5 despite the drawn order,
since B8 statistics are written directly onto an existing
\code{lake\_features} row, so it can be fetched before, after, or
concurrently with embedding generation.

exists, it falls back to the Level-1C collection (Table~\ref{tab:collection_strategy}). This decision is made before the export
request and is recorded in the processing metadata.

Scenes are filtered before compositing: the deployed configuration
excludes those whose reported cloud cover exceeds $20\%$
(\code{GEE\_CLOUD\_COVER\_MAX}) and masks individual unusable pixels
using cloud-probability and class-based information where available
--- collection-aware, since early Sentinel-2 products expose
different quality bands (\code{QA60}, \code{MSK\_CLASSI\_OPAQUE},
\code{MSK\_CLASSI\_CIRRUS}) than later ones. The filtered
observations are then combined into an annual per-pixel, per-band
median composite,

\begin{equation}
I_{annual}(p,b)
=
median\left(
I_1(p,b),I_2(p,b),\ldots,I_n(p,b)
\right)
\end{equation}

clipped to the requested extent, and exported as local GeoTIFF at
10 m resolution in floating-point form, so the same composite never
needs recomputing for a later analysis cell. Each tile carries status
metadata (lake ID, year, tile index, bounding box, local path), and
the pipeline checks this before starting a new Earth Engine request
for the same tile --- concrete, tile-level idempotency
(Section~\ref{sec:data_fetch_workflow}): a batch interrupted partway
through a lake resumes from its first incomplete tile.

\section{Step 11: Model Input Preparation}

Each retained analysis cell is read from the local merged GeoTIFF and
the six selected bands are converted into the model's expected
tensor layout, numerical range, and spatial dimensions. Separating
geographic geometry, raster extraction, model preprocessing, and
model inference into distinct stages makes it possible to change the
model without re-downloading the underlying satellite data.

\section{Step 12: Prithvi Embedding Generation}

Prithvi is used as the geospatial foundation model: each retained
analysis cell, as a six-band \code{B2--B7} image $X$, is transformed
into a fixed-length feature vector $f(X) \rightarrow \mathbf{z} \in
\mathbb{R}^{768}$, batched eight at a time

vector is L2-normalised, so that for unit-norm vectors the cosine
similarity reduces exactly to the inner product and the entire
similarity search can be executed as matrix multiplication.

\section{Step 12a: Coverage-Weighted Lake-Level Pooling}
\label{sec:pooling}

Cell-level embeddings preserve spatial detail, but retrieval and
forecasting operate on lakes, so for a lake-year with cells
$c_1,\dots,c_N$ having embeddings $\mathbf{e}_i$ and lake-coverage
percentages $w_i$, the lake embedding is the coverage-weighted mean,
re-normalised:

\begin{equation}
\mathbf{z}_{lake}
=
\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \mathbf{e}_i}
{\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \mathbf{e}_i\right\|_2}
\end{equation}

so that a cell almost entirely filled by the lake contributes more
than one that merely clips its margin; cells with zero coverage,
zero vectors, or the wrong dimensionality are excluded before
pooling. Across the cohort this reduces \textbf{233{,}002} cell
embeddings to \textbf{8{,}227} lake-year vectors, an average of
\textbf{28.3} cells per lake-year, and the cell-level vectors are
retained as the underlying evidence for any result the pooled vector
produces.

\section{Step 15: Trajectory Similarity Search}
\label{sec:trajectory}

Comparing one query embedding against one candidate embedding asks
only \emph{which lake looked like this lake, in this year?} --- too
weak a question for forecasting, since two lakes can coincide in a
single year and diverge over the following decade. EcoTwin instead
asks which lake has been \textbf{moving through} the representation
space the way this lake has been moving: a shared five-year path is
a far better basis for projecting the next three.

For a query lake with embeddings ordered by year, EcoTwin forms the
trajectory ending at anchor year $t_0$ with window size $W=5$,

\begin{equation}
Q = \left(\mathbf{q}_{t_0-4},\ \mathbf{q}_{t_0-3},\
\mathbf{q}_{t_0-2},\ \mathbf{q}_{t_0-1},\ \mathbf{q}_{t_0}\right)
\end{equation}

and each candidate lake with at least $W$ years of history is
expanded into every contiguous five-year window (ten years of data
yields six windows),

\begin{equation}
C^{(j)} = \left(\mathbf{c}_{y_j},\dots,\mathbf{c}_{y_j+4}\right),
\qquad j = 1,\dots,\,n-W+1
\end{equation}

which need not align in calendar time with the query: a lake that
underwent in 2017--2021 the transition another lake is undergoing in
2021--2025 is precisely the analog sought, since it has already
lived through the target's near future. Each window is scored as the
mean of the year-by-year cosine similarities between corresponding
positions,

\begin{equation}
S\!\left(Q, C^{(j)}\right)
=
\frac{1}{W}
\sum_{i=1}^{W}
\frac{\mathbf{q}_i^{\top}\mathbf{c}^{(j)}_i}
{\|\mathbf{q}_i\|_2\,\|\mathbf{c}^{(j)}_i\|_2}
\end{equation}

which (all embeddings being unit-norm) reduces to a mean of inner
products, and each candidate lake is represented by its single best
window,

\begin{equation}
S^{*}_{lake} = \max_{j} S\!\left(Q, C^{(j)}\right),
\qquad
j^{*} = \arg\max_{j} S\!\left(Q, C^{(j)}\right)
\end{equation}

whose end year $y_{j^{*}}+4$ is the pivot on which the forecasting
layer turns: the analog's observed behaviour \emph{after} that year
is the evidence used to project the target's future.

Because every vector is unit-norm and drawn from one model, raw
cosine scores cluster tightly just below $1.0$ (measured top analogs
span roughly 0.986--0.999 in Chapter~\ref{chap:ramsar}), which would
make every analog read as ``99\% similar'' if displayed directly.
EcoTwin therefore linearly rescales $[\phi, 1.0] \to [0,1]$ with
floor $\phi=0.5$,

\begin{equation}
S_{display} = \mathrm{clip}_{[0,1]}\!\left(\frac{S^{*}-\phi}{1-\phi}\right)
\end{equation}

a monotone transform that changes no ranking; raw scores are
reported alongside the rescaled ones in Chapter~\ref{chap:ramsar} so
the distinction stays visible. Two further metrics are implemented
and selectable per query (Euclidean distance mapped by $1/(1+d)$,
and inner-product mapped by $(\langle
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\rangle+1)/2$), though cosine is the default
used throughout this report.

The current implementation compares in Python across all candidate
windows: with $L$ lakes, $n$ years, and dimension $d$, the cost is
$O(L\cdot(n{-}W{+}1)\cdot W\cdot d)$, roughly $829\times6\times5\times768
\approx 1.9\times10^7$ multiply-accumulates per query --- measured
at \textbf{141--172\,ms} in Chapter~\ref{chap:ramsar}, comfortably
interactive but linear in lake count, which motivates the pgvector
ANN migration under future work.

\section{Step 16: Temporal Profile Generation}

For the query lake and its retrieved analogs, the stored band
statistics are assembled into per-year index series using the
formulas of Table~\ref{tab:indices}, computed on demand from the
persisted band means (the single source of truth for measured
reflectance) rather than stored pre-computed, so a change to an
index definition takes effect everywhere at once without a database
migration.

\section{Step 17: Directional Ecological Forecasting}
\label{sec:forecast_method}

The forecasting layer transfers \emph{measured change} from the
analogs onto the target, presented here in implementation order. The
most recent completed feature row for the target lake fixes the
anchor year $t_0$ and anchor values $v_k(t_0)$ for each index $k$;
forecasts are produced for $t_0+1,t_0+2,t_0+3$ (\code{\_FORECAST\_HORIZON}).
The trajectory search of Section~\ref{sec:trajectory} then runs with
an enlarged candidate pool, walked in rank order: a candidate is
admitted as a \emph{twin} only if it is a different lake than the
target, has a completed feature row at its matched window end year,
and has at least one usable year after that, so the five twins that
emerge are the five best \emph{usable} analogs rather than merely
the five highest-scoring rows.

For twin $i$ with matched window ending at year $m_i$, the change
transferred at offset $h$ is $\delta_{i,k,h} = v_{i,k}(m_i+h) -
v_{i,k}(m_i)$ --- not the analog's absolute index value, which
reflects its own climate, geology, and catchment, but the
\emph{change} it underwent, comparable across lakes in a way
absolute values are not. Twins receive fixed weights by rank rather
than weights derived from their similarity scores,

\begin{equation}
\omega_1 = 0.28,\quad
\omega_2 = 0.24,\quad
\omega_3 = 0.20,\quad
\omega_4 = 0.16,\quad
\omega_5 = 0.12
\end{equation}

precisely because raw similarity scores compress into a narrow band
near $1.0$ (Section~\ref{sec:trajectory}), where score-proportional
weights would be nearly uniform and discard the ranking the search
worked to establish. Twins do not all have data at every offset, so
with $\mathcal{A}_{k,h}$ the set of twins with a usable delta for
index $k$ at offset $h$, weights are renormalised over exactly that
set,

\begin{equation}
\tilde{\omega}_{i,k,h}
=
\frac{\omega_i}{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{A}_{k,h}} \omega_j},
\qquad i \in \mathcal{A}_{k,h}
\end{equation}

so the denominator is always $1.0$ and a year with only two twins
does not silently shrink toward ``stable''; the twin count is
reported separately as confidence. The score for index $k$ at offset
$h$ is

\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{k,h}
=
\sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}_{k,h}}
\tilde{\omega}_{i,k,h}\,\delta_{i,k,h}
\end{equation}

classified against threshold $\tau=0.005$:

\begin{equation}
D_{k,h} =
\begin{cases}
\text{uncertain (?)} & |\mathcal{A}_{k,h}| < 2\\[4pt]
\text{up } (\uparrow)   & \Sigma_{k,h} > +\tau\\[2pt]
\text{down } (\downarrow) & \Sigma_{k,h} < -\tau\\[2pt]
\text{stable } (\rightarrow) & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

with \emph{uncertain} checked first and kept distinct from
\emph{stable}: stable is a positive finding that the twins agree
little changes, while uncertain admits too few twins had data to
support any claim, and Chapter~\ref{chap:ramsar} contains real cases
where this distinction carries the entire meaning of a result. An
indicative magnitude $\hat{v}_k(t_0+h) = v_k(t_0) + \Sigma_{k,h}$
accompanies each direction, reported as an expected value rather
than a calibrated point prediction. Every forecast returns with a
complete audit report --- anchor values, weight scheme,
classification rule, and each twin's identity, matched window, and
weighted contribution --- so any published direction can be
recomputed by hand from the reported numbers.

Unlike a parametric time-series model, whose prediction runs through
learned parameters difficult to connect to a particular ecosystem,
EcoTwin provides an explicit, inspectable chain: Target
$\rightarrow$ Retrieved Twin $\rightarrow$ Similarity $\rightarrow$
Historical Trajectory $\rightarrow$ Projected Trajectory. There is
no learned parameter anywhere in the method above; every quantity is
either a measured reflectance-derived index or a fixed, disclosed
constant ($\omega_{1..5}$ and $\tau$), and an interval-based
uncertainty measure (the spread across twin predictions) was
considered and rejected in favour of the simpler twin count
$|\mathcal{A}_{k,h}|$, since a naive dispersion measure over a
small, unevenly-weighted set --- as when Vembanad's 2027 red-edge
score was dominated by one twin with an extreme delta
(Chapter~\ref{chap:ramsar}) --- is not a stable confidence signal,
while the count drops to zero exactly when a forecast should not be
trusted and degrades gracefully otherwise.

\subsection{Worked Example: Chilika, NDVI-B7, $h=1$}

Chapter~\ref{chap:ramsar} reported Chilika's 2026 NDVI-B7 forecast as
$\uparrow$ with score $+0.0244$ from five twins. Reconstructing it:
all five twins have a usable year at $h=1$, so no renormalization is
needed beyond the base weights $(0.28,0.24,0.20,0.16,0.12)$, which
already sum to 1. Each twin's delta is its measured NDVI-B7 one year
after its own matched-window end, minus its value at that end year
--- five independent measurements from five different lakes and
calendar years, combined only through their rank weight. The result,
$+0.0244$, exceeds $\tau=0.005$, giving $\uparrow$, and every
intermediate quantity is recoverable from the audit report returned
alongside the forecast.

\subsection{Hindcasting Protocol}
\label{sec:hindcast_protocol}

A rigorous accuracy evaluation requires hindcasting: hide each
lake's final $h_{max}$ years, run the identical retrieval and
forecast pipeline using only years before the cutoff, and score the
forecast against the held-out observations. For cutoff year $t_c \in
\{2019,\dots,2022\}$, both the query trajectory and every
candidate's embedding history are restricted to years $\le t_c$;
the method then runs exactly as deployed, producing
$\hat{v}_k(t_c+h)$ for $h=1,2,3$, compared against the actual
recorded $v_k(t_c+h)$ already present in \code{lake\_features}. The
protocol reports, per index and per horizon (never pooled, since
scales differ by an order of magnitude or more), both a magnitude
metric and a \emph{directional hit rate}:

\begin{equation}
MAE_{k,h} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|v_{k,i}(t_c+h) - \hat{v}_{k,i}(t_c+h)\right|
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
HitRate_{k,h} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{1}\!\left[\mathrm{sign}(\Sigma) = \mathrm{sign}(\Delta_{actual})\right]
\end{equation}

Running this at $t_c=2022$ requires three held-out years
(2023--2025) plus five years of prior history --- exactly the ten
years this project has, so a single cutoff gives only one evaluation
point per lake: too coarse a sample for a defensible aggregate
accuracy figure. Multiple cutoff years are not yet available because
the dataset spans only one decade, so this protocol is documented
here as the immediate next step (Section~\ref{sec:future}) rather
than executed prematurely on insufficient data.

\section{PostgreSQL, PostGIS, and pgvector}

PostgreSQL is the central persistent store. PostGIS keeps the
original lake geometry, analysis-cell geometry, and spatial metadata
in the database itself rather than converted into plain
latitude/longitude tables, so intersection, containment, area, and
map-query operations run directly as SQL. The pgvector extension
stores the 768-dimensional Prithvi vectors alongside this spatial
data, maintaining the association Embedding
$\leftrightarrow$ Subregion $\leftrightarrow$ Lake $\leftrightarrow$
Year so that cosine-distance/similarity operations execute directly
against the indexed database rather than exporting vectors to CSV or
recomputing features.

\section{Backend and Frontend}

The FastAPI backend provides services for lake metadata lookup,
nearest lake/grid-cell lookup, analog similarity search, temporal
feature retrieval, forecast retrieval, report generation, and
health/system status, separating cheap data retrieval from heavy
batch processing so long operations are handled asynchronously.

The frontend is implemented using React, Vite, and TypeScript, with
the map on Leaflet/React-Leaflet. A user selects or searches for a
lake, views its geographic boundary and metadata, requests analogous
ecosystems, visualizes their locations and similarity scores, and
inspects their historical profiles alongside the forecast. Displayed
lake boundaries and analog markers are drawn from the same PostGIS
geometry used for the analysis itself, rather than a separately
derived copy, which avoids the common failure mode where the
analytical result and the displayed map disagree due to different
coordinate transformations or numbering systems.

\section{Asynchronous Processing and Progress Tracking}

Imagery download, raster merging, feature extraction, and model
inference all take far longer than an acceptable HTTP request, so
each is launched through FastAPI's \code{BackgroundTasks} and
returns immediately. A distributed task queue was considered and not
adopted: the pipelines are operator-run batch jobs on a single
machine, and the durable state a queue would provide already exists
in the database, where every tile, image, cell, and feature row
carries its own status. Progress is instead exposed through
module-level state guarded by a \code{threading.Lock} (processed,
skipped, and failed counts, elapsed time, throughput, projected
completion), with each pipeline's block independent so concurrent
runs report separately; the frontend polls the corresponding
\code{/status} endpoint. Long jobs are also cancellable: a flag
checked between work units means a cancelled run stops after the
current lake-year rather than mid-write, and completed work stays
committed.

\section{Server-Side B8 Acquisition}
\label{sec:b8}

Unlike \code{B2--B7}, which must be downloaded as pixels for Prithvi
inference, B8 is needed only as summary statistics, so moving pixels
at all would be wasted work. The B8 pipeline instead performs the
reduction entirely on Earth Engine's servers using
\code{Image.reduceRegions()}: lake polygons are batched fifteen at a
time into a \code{FeatureCollection} and reduced against a single
annual composite (reused across every lake in that year) with a
combined mean/stdDev/median/minMax reducer, so only the resulting
numbers are transferred and no imagery is ever downloaded. Transient
failures are retried three times with exponential backoff, and a
separate \code{b8\_status} column tracks this pipeline independently
so a B8 failure never marks the underlying feature row as failed ---
completed for all \textbf{8{,}227} lake-years in the cohort.

Measured raw cosine scores across all five queries fall between
\textbf{0.9969} and \textbf{0.9987}, with the tenth-ranked analog
typically within \textbf{0.005} of the first --- expected given that
every vector shares one encoder and describes the same general
scene type (Section~\ref{sec:trajectory}), so \emph{the absolute
score is close to uninformative; only the ordering carries signal}.
A similarity score therefore indicates closeness in the learned
representation space, not identical ecological processes; ecological
interpretation requires additional evidence from lake metadata,
spatial maps, and temporal environmental indicators (Chapter~\ref{chap:ramsar}).
A meaningful absolute scale would require calibration against
labelled ecosystem pairs (Section~\ref{sec:future}).

\section{Temporal Profile Results}

The temporal layer provides a second, independent axis of
validation alongside spatial similarity: EcoTwin presents, for the
query lake and its analogs, the full ten-year series of all five
implemented indices (Table~\ref{tab:indices}), observation
availability, and the directional forecast with its contributing
twins. Its value is demonstrated concretely by Wular Lake in
Chapter~\ref{chap:ramsar}, whose geographically unrelated top
analogs nonetheless share its independently measured eutrophication
trend.

\section{Processing Reliability}

The system was intentionally designed to tolerate partial failure. A
lake-year can be pending, processing, completed, failed, or skipped
because its output already exists, which makes large multi-year
extraction jobs restartable instead of requiring a complete restart
after a single failed Earth Engine request.

\section{Engineering Problems Encountered}

Several recurring problems shaped the implementation. Large Earth
Engine requests sometimes produced memory-limit errors, solved by
reducing the size of each export region and moving part of the
processing to local raster operations; parallel workers separately
caused HTTP 429 and connection-pool saturation errors when too many
requests were issued simultaneously, which pushed the pipeline
toward smaller controlled worker counts, resumable jobs, and local
reuse of already-generated imagery. The early Sentinel-2 Level-1C
fallback exposed a different set of quality bands than other
collections --- requests that selected \code{MSK\_CLASSI\_CIRRUS},
\code{MSK\_CLASSI\_OPAQUE}, or \code{QA60} could fail depending on
the actual image schema --- which led to collection-aware
preprocessing rather than a single hard-coded cloud-mask
implementation. Merged rasters can also become much larger than
individual source tiles, making compression, floating-point type
control, tiled GeoTIFF writing, and local reuse important; at one
point during development the local root filesystem reached 100\%
utilization, causing raster operations to fail with ``No space left
on device'' and demonstrating that the pipeline must account for
intermediate storage, not only final dataset size. Finally, Earth
Engine authentication and access depend on external network
services, and authentication failures or unreachable OAuth endpoints
temporarily prevented pipeline execution --- so the implementation
separates authentication from per-lake processing and records
failures instead of silently producing incomplete datasets.

Because the project was developed by multiple team members, a
database consolidation procedure was also designed: each developer
creates a PostgreSQL custom-format dump, which is restored into a
temporary database, validated through a Python ETL process, and then
merged into the production database. The procedure preserves PostGIS
geometries, pgvector embeddings, indexes, constraints, and
PostgreSQL data types, which is safer than CSV-based migration
because CSV does not naturally preserve PostGIS geometry and vector
types.

\textbf{The embedding space encodes real ecological structure}: the

\chapter{Discussion}
\label{chap:discussion}

\section{Strengths and Limitations}

The system's central strengths, established in earlier chapters, are
scalable offline preprocessing executed to completion with zero
residual failures (Chapter~\ref{chap:results}); trajectory-based
retrieval that matches ecological \emph{condition} rather than
single-year appearance, demonstrated by the Tso Morari--Pangong
pairing (Chapter~\ref{chap:ramsar}); and a decoder-free, fully
auditable forecasting layer that reports \emph{uncertain} rather
than guessing when evidence is thin (a 13.3\% abstention rate across
75 forecast cells).

Its limitations are correspondingly structural. The ten-year window
is not uniformly complete (Table~\ref{tab:year_coverage}), and
Sentinel-2's optical sensing means cloud cover reduces valid
observations (mean 64.4\% before masking). A high embedding
similarity does not guarantee ecological equivalence --- only the
\emph{ranking} is meaningful, not the compressed absolute score
(Chapter~\ref{chap:results}) --- and every result is conditional on
the fixed configuration of Table~\ref{tab:current_config}, not a
configuration-independent measurement. The clearest limitation is
that a twin whose matched window ends near 2025 has little observed
future to transfer, which emptied Loktak's $h=3$ forecast and
thinned Vembanad's (Chapter~\ref{chap:ramsar}) --- not a flaw in the
classification rule, which correctly reported \emph{uncertain}, but
a consequence of dataset extent that self-corrects as further years
are ingested. Finally, the Ramsar case studies were checked against
publicly documented site condition rather than a quantitative field
survey, which is corroborating evidence, not calibrated validation;
formal hindcasting (Section~\ref{sec:hindcast_protocol}) is the next
validation step precisely because it does not require such external
data.

\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{chap:conclusion}

\section{Conclusion}

EcoTwin evolved from a conceptual ecosystem-comparison idea into a
complete, fully executed geospatial retrieval and forecasting
system: HydroLAKES and Sentinel-2 feed a buffered, tiled acquisition
pipeline into Prithvi embeddings, coverage-pooled to lake level and
indexed in pgvector, from which a five-year sliding-window
trajectory search ranks analogs and a decoder-free forecasting layer
transfers their measured index changes onto the target with a full
audit trail --- executed to completion over 829 lakes, ten years,
and zero outstanding failures (Chapter~\ref{chap:results}).

Chapter~\ref{chap:ramsar} demonstrated the complete pipeline on five
Ramsar wetlands, returning a striking unsupervised validation (Tso
Morari's top analog is Pangong Tso, the only other high-altitude
Ladakhi lake in the cohort), an independently corroborated
degradation signal at Wular, and, equally important, a forecasting
method that correctly declined to predict in 10 of 75 cells where
evidence was insufficient rather than manufacturing a confident
answer. The project's most significant contribution is therefore
the integration of geospatial data engineering, foundation-model
inference, vector retrieval, temporal analysis, and visualization
into one reproducible workflow carried through to applied results,
demonstrating along the way --- through the abandoned
embedding-decoder design (Section~\ref{sec:pooling}) --- that
transferring measured evidence is often more defensible than
reconstructing it from an uncalibrated inversion.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future}

Priority items are executing the hindcasting protocol
(Section~\ref{sec:hindcast_protocol}) once further years extend the
dataset past its current single-cutoff limitation; extending
ingestion beyond 2025 so recently-matched twins accumulate the
future years needed to resolve \emph{uncertain} cells; adding
independent field-ecological validation for the Ramsar sites; and
migrating trajectory search to pgvector's native ANN operators,
since the current brute-force scan will not remain interactive as
the cohort grows. Further directions include calibrating the
cosine-similarity display scale against labelled ecosystem pairs,
attention- or learned-weighted pooling in place of coverage-weighted
mean, growing the active cohort beyond 829 lakes, reducing
monsoon-driven cloud gaps in temporal compositing, and deploying
shared production infrastructure for multi-user access.

\section{Final Remarks}

EcoTwin's central idea is to use historically observed ecosystems as
computationally retrievable analogs rather than treating every
ecosystem as an isolated forecasting problem. The Ramsar case
studies show that this idea, carried through a fully processed
829-lake, ten-year cohort, produces results that are specific,
checkable against independent knowledge of the sites, and ---
where the evidence ran out --- honest about the limits of what it
can currently claim.
