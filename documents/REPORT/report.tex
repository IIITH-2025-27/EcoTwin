\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

EcoTwin is an end-to-end geospatial ecosystem analog discovery
platform designed around the question: \emph{which ecosystems
elsewhere exhibit similar spatial structure, and what can their
historical trajectories tell us about a target ecosystem?} HydroLAKES
polygons provide ecosystem boundaries; a buffered, tiled Sentinel-2
acquisition pipeline through Google Earth Engine yields
one-kilometre analysis cells, converted into six-band Prithvi
embeddings, coverage-pooled to one 768-dimensional lake-year vector
and indexed in PostgreSQL/pgvector. A query ecosystem is compared as
a \emph{five-year trajectory} rather than a single snapshot, sliding
across each candidate's history to find its best-matching window,
and the ranked results are exposed through an interactive map and
profile views with historical index trajectories.

The deployed system holds a complete, fully processed cohort ---
\textbf{829 active lakes across 26 Indian states}, ten consecutive
years (2016--2025), \textbf{8{,}227 lake-year embeddings},
\textbf{19{,}046} imagery tiles, and \textbf{233{,}002} analysis
cells, with no outstanding failures at any pipeline stage. The
forecasting layer is analog-based and non-parametric: rather than
decoding an embedding back into reflectance, it uses the embedding
space only to \emph{rank} analogs, then transfers their measured
year-on-year changes onto the target as a rank-weighted directional
forecast, traceable to the specific lakes, years, and deltas that
produced it. The system is demonstrated on five Ramsar-designated
wetlands and reservoirs --- Chilika, Maharana Pratap Sagar
(Beas/Pong Dam), Tawa Reservoir, Tso Morari, and Sasthamcotta ---
reported in full in Chapter~\ref{chap:ramsar}.

\textbf{Keywords:}
Geospatial Foundation Models, Prithvi, Remote Sensing, Sentinel-2,
HydroLAKES, Ecosystem Analogs, Vector Search, pgvector, NDCI,
Red-Edge Indices, Ramsar Wetlands, Trajectory Similarity

\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chap:introduction}

\section{Background and Problem Statement}

Earth observation satellites provide repeated measurements of
ecosystems at scales infeasible through field observation alone, and
Sentinel-2's multispectral imagery suits vegetation, water, and
surface-condition analysis. Rather than beginning with a specific
question such as vegetation change, EcoTwin treats ecosystem
comparison as a retrieval problem: satellite observations are
converted into learned feature vectors and indexed, so a lake can be
compared against a large reference collection without reprocessing
raw imagery each time, asking whether another ecosystem looks
similar in its spatial-spectral representation.

Several engineering constraints shape the solution: lake boundaries
are irregular polygons rather than fixed-size squares, so large
ecosystems require multiple image tiles; historical Sentinel-2
collections do not expose identical quality bands across all years,
so cloud masking must be collection-aware; every embedding must stay
associated with its source geometry and lake/year metadata; and
similarity search must be substantially cheaper than recomputing
embeddings from scratch.

\section{Core EcoTwin Hypothesis}

EcoTwin is based on the operational hypothesis:

\begin{quote}
\emph{Ecosystems elsewhere that are structurally similar in
geospatial feature space can provide useful analogs for understanding
the spatial and historical behaviour of a target ecosystem.}
\end{quote}

The hypothesis separates two tasks. The first is \textbf{analog
discovery}: finding ecosystems that resemble the target in the
learned spatial-spectral representation. The second is
\textbf{trajectory analysis}: comparing the historical environmental
indicators of the target against those of its discovered analogs.

\section{Project Evolution and Contributions}

The implementation evolved from standardized $5\times5$ km ecosystem
blocks toward a lake-centred buffered imagery stage, local raster
merging, and $1\times1$ km analysis cells, letting large lakes be
processed without forcing the foundation model onto irregular
polygons. A further pivot shaped the final system:
\textbf{snapshot similarity gave way to trajectory similarity} ---
comparing single embeddings proved too weak a notion of resemblance,
since two lakes can coincide in one year and diverge over a decade,
so the system instead compares a five-year trajectory against every
sliding window in each candidate's history
(Section~\ref{sec:trajectory}).

The project's contribution is the resulting end-to-end
lake-to-embedding-to-analog pipeline, executed over an 829-lake,
ten-year cohort with no residual failures: Earth-Engine-based
satellite acquisition with polygon-aware subdivision; Prithvi-based
six-band representations with coverage-weighted pooling, persisted
as 768-dimensional vectors; the forecasting method above; a
band-honest ecological index set; and a FastAPI/React-Leaflet
application with automated PDF reporting, evaluated on five
Ramsar-designated wetlands and reservoirs.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

EcoTwin combines three established but, to the authors' knowledge,
not previously combined threads. \textbf{Analog-based forecasting}
is classically meteorological: Lorenz (1969) matched historical
pressure-field states as analog pairs, and van den Dool (1989)
extended this to operational forecasting while noting how vast a
library true analogs require --- the same problem EcoTwin's
trajectory matching and cross-lake pooling (828 candidate histories,
not one lake's own past) are structural attempts to work
around.\footnote{E. N. Lorenz, ``Atmospheric Predictability as
Revealed by Naturally Occurring Analogues,'' \textit{J. Atmos.
Sci.}, 26(4), 1969; H. M. van den Dool, ``A New Look at Weather
Forecasting through Analogues,'' \textit{Mon. Wea. Rev.}, 117(10),
1989.} \textbf{Geospatial foundation models}: Prithvi-EO-2.0 reports
state-of-the-art downstream performance across benchmark
tasks,\footnote{D. Szwarcman \textit{et al.}, ``Prithvi-EO-2.0,''
arXiv:2412.02732, 2024.} and recent work applying Google DeepMind's
AlphaEarth embeddings to wetland classification independently
validates the premise that a modern GeoFM's embedding space encodes
ecologically meaningful structure exploitable by similarity
operations alone, without forecasting or trajectory
matching.\footnote{S. Ryan \textit{et al.}, ``Streamlining Wetland
Vegetation Mapping with AlphaEarth Embeddings,'' \textit{Remote
Sensing}, 18(2), 2026.} \textbf{Index validation}: NDCI, EcoTwin's
chlorophyll-a proxy, is corroborated by a 2026 review of 122
Sentinel-2 chlorophyll-a studies reporting good agreement in
eutrophic waters but reduced reliability in clear, oligotrophic
water such as Tso Morari;\footnote{S. Goodrich \textit{et al.},
``Sentinel-2 for chlorophyll-a water quality monitoring,''
\textit{Int. J. Remote Sens.}, 2026.} NDVI and NDWI rest on the
older, widely-adopted vegetation-index literature already in this
report's bibliography. None of this validates EcoTwin's forecasts
directly --- that requires the hindcasting protocol of
Section~\ref{sec:hindcast_protocol} --- but it grounds the two
components the forecast is built from: analog reasoning as an
established paradigm, and the foundation model and index formulas as
independently vetted.

\section{Overview}

and metadata) with repeated satellite observations (Sentinel-2): the
static layer defines \emph{where} an ecosystem is, the satellite

geometry, area, elevation, pour-point, type, depth, volume, and

pipeline was narrowed from the full HydroLAKES inventory to a

\section{Environmental Indices: A Band-Honest Index Set}

The merged lake imagery used for Prithvi inference contains only
\code{B2--B7}, not B8 (broad NIR), so the textbook NDVI/NDWI forms
could not initially be computed. EcoTwin substituted the narrow-NIR
red-edge channel B7 for B8 and recorded the substitution in the
index names (NDVI-B7) rather than concealing it. That gap has since
closed: the server-side B8 pipeline of Section~\ref{sec:b8} now
populates \code{lake\_features.b8\_mean} for every lake-year
(\textbf{8{,}227/8{,}227}) without downloading a raster, so NDVI and
NDWI are now computed against true NIR; NDCI, Turbidity, and
Red-Edge Slope never needed NIR and are unaffected. All five indices
are computed from per-band \emph{mean} reflectance over the lake
polygon: NDCI ($\frac{B5-B4}{B5+B4}$) for chlorophyll-a/algal bloom;
NDVI ($\frac{B8-B4}{B8+B4}$) for vegetation vigor, tracking
macrophytes and floating weed; NDWI ($\frac{B3-B8}{B3+B8}$), the
standard McFeeters water index, falling as vegetation raises B8;
Turbidity ($\frac{B4-B3}{B4+B3}$) for suspended sediment; and
Red-Edge Slope ($\frac{B7-B5}{78\text{nm}}$) for pigment
concentration and canopy density.

\chapter{Detailed EcoTwin Methodology}
\label{chap:methodology}

\section{End-to-End Pipeline}

The implemented workflow is:

\begin{quote}
\small\centering
Lake Inventory $\rightarrow$ Boundary Validation $\rightarrow$
Buffering $\rightarrow$ Imagery Tiling $\rightarrow$ Sentinel-2
Composite $\rightarrow$ GeoTIFF $\rightarrow$ Local Mosaic
$\rightarrow$ 1\,km Cells $\rightarrow$ Coverage Filter $\rightarrow$
Prithvi $\rightarrow$ 768D Embeddings $\rightarrow$ pgvector
$\rightarrow$ Cosine Search $\rightarrow$ Analogs $\rightarrow$
Temporal Profiles $\rightarrow$ Forecast
\end{quote}

\label{sec:data_fetch_workflow}

Figure~\ref{fig:data_fetch_workflow} shows how an operator actually
\emph{runs} it, as seven sequential stages, each a separate API
call, background job, and database write, so a failure at stage 4
leaves stages 1--3 intact and does not require re-fetching imagery
already on disk.

\label{sec:step2_buffering}

Steps 1--2 (Boundary Retrieval and Buffering): the pipeline reads
the target lake polygon from PostGIS, validates
it, and transforms it into a projected CRS, then expands it with a
configurable buffer (\SI{1200}{\meter} during development), since
the ecosystem representation is not restricted to the water polygon
itself: surrounding shoreline and vegetation contribute to the
spatial-spectral signature.

\label{sec:step3_grid}

Step 3 (Projected Grid Generation): the buffered polygon is
projected into a metric CRS, and a regular
imagery grid --- deliberately coarser than the final analysis grid,
at an \SI{8500}{\meter} tile size chosen to balance Earth Engine
request size, memory use, and local processing cost --- is generated
around it. A lake may therefore produce one, two, or several imagery
tiles depending on its size and shape.

Steps 4--8 (Sentinel-2 Compositing, Export, and Resumability): for
each lake-year combination, the pipeline first queries the
preferred surface-reflectance collection (\code{S2\_SR\_HARMONIZED});
if no usable observation exists, it falls back to the Level-1C
collection (\code{S2}), a decision recorded in the processing
metadata before export. Scenes with cloud cover exceeding $20\%$ are
excluded, and unusable pixels are masked using cloud-probability and
class-based information where available. The filtered observations
are then combined into an annual per-pixel, per-band median
composite,

\begin{equation}
I_{annual}(p,b)
=
median\left(
I_1(p,b),I_2(p,b),\ldots,I_n(p,b)
\right)
\end{equation}

clipped to the requested extent, and exported as local GeoTIFF at
10 m resolution. Each tile carries status metadata (lake ID, year,
tile index, bounding box, local path), checked before starting a new
Earth Engine request for the same tile: a batch interrupted partway
through a lake resumes from its first incomplete tile.

\label{sec:step9_merge}

Step 9 (Local Raster Merging): after all tiles belonging to a
lake-year are available, rasterio
merges them into a single raster mosaic, reprojecting and writing
with compression settings that reduce storage while preserving the
numerical precision inference needs. The merged image becomes the
stable source from which analysis cells are extracted.

\section{Embedding Generation: Analysis Cells through Pooling}

Step 10 (Analysis-Cell Generation): the merged lake image is
intersected with a regular $1\times1$ km
analysis grid, and for each cell the lake coverage is computed,

\begin{equation}
Coverage(c)
=
\frac{Area(c\cap Lake)}
{Area(c)}
\times 100
\end{equation}

with only cells above a $15\%$ threshold retained, preventing cells
that merely touch a lake's corner from being treated as
representative.

\label{sec:step12_prithvi}

Steps 11--12 (Model Input Preparation and Prithvi Embedding
Generation): each retained analysis cell is read from the local
merged GeoTIFF and
its six selected bands are converted into the model's expected
tensor layout, numerical range, and spatial dimensions --- kept as a
distinct stage from inference so the model can be changed without
re-downloading satellite data. Prithvi, the geospatial foundation
model, then transforms each six-band \code{B2--B7} cell image $X$
into a fixed-length feature vector $f(X) \rightarrow \mathbf{z} \in
\mathbb{R}^{768}$, batched eight at a time
(\code{EMBEDDING\_GPU\_BATCH\_SIZE}) on CPU inference. Every output
vector is L2-normalised, so cosine similarity reduces exactly to the
inner product and the entire similarity search can be executed as
matrix multiplication.

\label{sec:pooling}

Step 12a (Coverage-Weighted Lake-Level Pooling): cell-level
embeddings preserve spatial detail, but retrieval and
forecasting operate on lakes. For a lake-year with cells
$c_1,\dots,c_N$ having embeddings $\mathbf{e}_i$ and lake-coverage
percentages $w_i$, the lake embedding is the coverage-weighted mean,
re-normalised:

\begin{equation}
\mathbf{z}_{lake}
=
\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \mathbf{e}_i}
{\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \mathbf{e}_i\right\|_2}
\end{equation}

so a cell almost entirely filled by the lake contributes more than
one that merely clips its margin; cells with zero coverage, zero
vectors, or the wrong dimensionality are excluded before pooling.
Across the cohort this reduces \textbf{233{,}002} cell embeddings to
\textbf{8{,}227} lake-year vectors, an average of \textbf{28.3} cells
per lake-year, with the cell-level vectors retained as the
underlying evidence for any result the pooled vector produces.

\section{Trajectory Similarity Search}
\label{sec:trajectory}

Comparing one query embedding against one candidate asks only
\emph{which lake looked like this lake, in this year?} --- too weak
a question for forecasting, since two lakes can coincide in a single
year and diverge over the following decade. EcoTwin instead asks
which lake has been moving through the representation space the way
this lake has been moving: a shared five-year path is a far better
basis for projecting the next three.

For a query lake with embeddings ordered by year, EcoTwin forms the
trajectory ending at anchor year $t_0$ with window size $W=5$,

\begin{equation}
Q = \left(\mathbf{q}_{t_0-4},\ \mathbf{q}_{t_0-3},\
\mathbf{q}_{t_0-2},\ \mathbf{q}_{t_0-1},\ \mathbf{q}_{t_0}\right)
\end{equation}

and each candidate lake with at least $W$ years of history is
expanded into every contiguous five-year window (ten years of data
yields six windows),

\begin{equation}
C^{(j)} = \left(\mathbf{c}_{y_j},\dots,\mathbf{c}_{y_j+4}\right),
\qquad j = 1,\dots,\,n-W+1
\end{equation}

which need not align in calendar time with the query: a lake that
underwent in 2017--2021 the transition another lake is undergoing in
2021--2025 is precisely the analog sought. Each window is scored as
the mean of the year-by-year cosine similarities between
corresponding positions,

\begin{equation}
S\!\left(Q, C^{(j)}\right)
=
\frac{1}{W}
\sum_{i=1}^{W}
\frac{\mathbf{q}_i^{\top}\mathbf{c}^{(j)}_i}
{\|\mathbf{q}_i\|_2\,\|\mathbf{c}^{(j)}_i\|_2}
\end{equation}

which reduces to a mean of inner products, and each candidate lake is
represented by its single best window,

\begin{equation}
S^{*}_{lake} = \max_{j} S\!\left(Q, C^{(j)}\right),
\qquad
j^{*} = \arg\max_{j} S\!\left(Q, C^{(j)}\right)
\end{equation}

whose end year $y_{j^{*}}+4$ is the pivot on which the forecasting
layer turns: the analog's observed behaviour \emph{after} that year
is the evidence used to project the target's future.

Because every vector is unit-norm and drawn from one model, raw
cosine scores cluster tightly just below $1.0$ (measured top analogs
span roughly 0.986--0.999 in Chapter~\ref{chap:ramsar}), which would
read as ``99\% similar'' if displayed directly. EcoTwin therefore
linearly rescales $[\phi, 1.0] \to [0,1]$ with floor $\phi=0.5$,

\begin{equation}
S_{display} = \mathrm{clip}_{[0,1]}\!\left(\frac{S^{*}-\phi}{1-\phi}\right)
\end{equation}

a monotone transform that changes no ranking; raw scores are
reported alongside the rescaled ones in Chapter~\ref{chap:ramsar} so
the distinction stays visible. Two further metrics are implemented
and selectable per query (Euclidean distance mapped by $1/(1+d)$,
and inner-product mapped by $(\langle
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\rangle+1)/2$), though cosine is the default.

The current implementation compares in Python across all candidate
windows: with $L$ lakes, $n$ years, and dimension $d$, the cost is
$O(L\cdot(n{-}W{+}1)\cdot W\cdot d)$, roughly $829\times6\times5\times768
\approx 1.9\times10^7$ multiply-accumulates per query --- measured
at \textbf{141--172\,ms} in Chapter~\ref{chap:ramsar}, comfortably
interactive but linear in lake count, motivating the pgvector ANN
migration under future work.

\section{Temporal Profiles and Directional Ecological Forecasting}
\label{sec:forecast_method}

Step 16 (Temporal Profile Generation): for the query lake and its
retrieved analogs, the stored band statistics are assembled into
per-year index series using the formulas of Table~\ref{tab:indices},
computed on demand from the persisted band means rather than stored
pre-computed, so a change to an index definition takes effect
everywhere at once.

Step 17 (Directional Ecological Forecasting): the forecasting layer
transfers \emph{measured change} from the
analogs onto the target. The most recent completed feature row for
the target lake fixes the anchor year $t_0$ and anchor values
$v_k(t_0)$ for each index $k$; forecasts are produced for
$t_0+1,t_0+2,t_0+3$. The trajectory search of
Section~\ref{sec:trajectory} then runs with an enlarged candidate
pool, walked in rank order: a candidate is admitted as a
\emph{twin} only if it is a different lake than the target, has a
completed feature row at its matched window end year, and has at
least one usable year after that, so the five twins that emerge are
the five best \emph{usable} analogs rather than merely the five
highest-scoring rows.

For twin $i$ with matched window ending at year $m_i$, the change
transferred at offset $h$ is $\delta_{i,k,h} = v_{i,k}(m_i+h) -
v_{i,k}(m_i)$ --- not the analog's absolute index value, which
reflects its own climate and catchment, but the \emph{change} it
underwent, comparable across lakes in a way absolute values are not.
Twins receive fixed weights by rank rather than weights derived from
similarity scores,

\begin{equation}
\omega_1 = 0.28,\quad
\omega_2 = 0.24,\quad
\omega_3 = 0.20,\quad
\omega_4 = 0.16,\quad
\omega_5 = 0.12
\end{equation}

precisely because raw similarity scores compress into a narrow band
near $1.0$ (Section~\ref{sec:trajectory}), where score-proportional
weights would be nearly uniform. Twins do not all have data at every
offset, so with $\mathcal{A}_{k,h}$ the set of twins with a usable
delta for index $k$ at offset $h$, weights are renormalised over
exactly that set,

\begin{equation}
\tilde{\omega}_{i,k,h}
=
\frac{\omega_i}{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{A}_{k,h}} \omega_j},
\qquad i \in \mathcal{A}_{k,h}
\end{equation}

so a year with only two twins does not silently shrink toward
``stable''; the twin count is reported separately as confidence. The
score for index $k$ at offset $h$ is

\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{k,h}
=
\sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}_{k,h}}
\tilde{\omega}_{i,k,h}\,\delta_{i,k,h}
\end{equation}

classified against threshold $\tau=0.005$:

\begin{equation}
D_{k,h} =
\begin{cases}
\text{uncertain (?)} & |\mathcal{A}_{k,h}| < 2\\[4pt]
\text{up } (\uparrow)   & \Sigma_{k,h} > +\tau\\[2pt]
\text{down } (\downarrow) & \Sigma_{k,h} < -\tau\\[2pt]
\text{stable } (\rightarrow) & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

with \emph{uncertain} checked first and kept distinct from
\emph{stable}: stable is a positive finding that the twins agree
little changes, while uncertain admits too few twins had data to
support any claim. An indicative magnitude $\hat{v}_k(t_0+h) =
v_k(t_0) + \Sigma_{k,h}$ accompanies each direction, reported as an
expected value rather than a calibrated point prediction. Every
forecast returns a complete audit report --- anchor values, weight
scheme, classification rule, and each twin's identity, matched
window, and weighted contribution --- so any published direction can
be recomputed by hand.

Unlike a parametric time-series model, whose prediction runs through
learned parameters difficult to connect to a particular ecosystem,
EcoTwin provides an explicit, inspectable chain: Target
$\rightarrow$ Retrieved Twin $\rightarrow$ Similarity $\rightarrow$
Historical Trajectory $\rightarrow$ Projected Trajectory, with no
learned parameter anywhere: every quantity is either a measured
reflectance-derived index or a fixed, disclosed constant
($\omega_{1..5}$ and $\tau$). An interval-based uncertainty measure
(the spread across twin predictions) was considered and rejected in
favour of the simpler twin count $|\mathcal{A}_{k,h}|$, since a naive
dispersion measure over a small, unevenly-weighted set is not a
stable confidence signal, while the count drops to zero exactly when
a forecast should not be trusted.

\subsection{Why Not an LSTM-Based Forecast?}
\label{sec:why_not_lstm}

A recurrent-neural-network forecast was considered and rejected.
Reddy and Prasad (2018) report strong LSTM accuracy for one-week
MODIS NDVI prediction, but trained on 861 weekly observations per
site.\footnote{D. Sushma Reddy and P. Rama Chandra Prasad,
``Prediction of vegetation dynamics using NDVI time series data and
LSTM,'' \emph{Modeling Earth Systems and Environment}, 2018,
\url{https://doi.org/10.1007/s40808-018-0431-3}.} EcoTwin has only
ten annual observations per lake across 829 lakes, a 1--3 year
horizon (not one week), and a requirement that every forecast be
traceable to specific evidence rather than trained weights --- three
conditions under which an LSTM would fit noise, cannot be
individually retrained per lake, and (like decoding a predicted
embedding, Section~\ref{sec:pooling}) offers no correspondence back
to a particular ecosystem. The rank-weighted transfer used instead
needs no training data beyond the twins themselves.

\subsection{Hindcasting Protocol}
\label{sec:hindcast_protocol}

A rigorous accuracy evaluation requires hindcasting: hide each
lake's final $h_{max}$ years, run the identical pipeline using only
years before the cutoff, and score the forecast against the
held-out observations. For cutoff year $t_c \in \{2019,\dots,2022\}$,
both the query trajectory and every candidate's embedding history
are restricted to years $\le t_c$, producing $\hat{v}_k(t_c+h)$ for
$h=1,2,3$ compared against the actual recorded $v_k(t_c+h)$. The
protocol reports, per index and per horizon, both a magnitude metric
and a directional hit rate:

\begin{equation}
MAE_{k,h} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|v_{k,i}(t_c+h) - \hat{v}_{k,i}(t_c+h)\right|
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
HitRate_{k,h} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{1}\!\left[\mathrm{sign}(\Sigma) = \mathrm{sign}(\Delta_{actual})\right]
\end{equation}

Running this at $t_c=2022$ requires three held-out years
(2023--2025) plus five years of prior history --- exactly the ten
years this project has, so a single cutoff gives only one evaluation
point per lake, too coarse for a defensible aggregate accuracy
figure. This protocol is therefore documented here as the immediate
next step (Section~\ref{sec:future}) rather than executed
prematurely on insufficient data.

\section{PostgreSQL, PostGIS, and pgvector}

PostgreSQL is the central persistent store. PostGIS keeps lake and
analysis-cell geometry in the database itself, so intersection,
containment, area, and map-query operations run directly as SQL. The
pgvector extension stores the 768-dimensional Prithvi vectors
alongside this spatial data, maintaining the association Embedding
$\leftrightarrow$ Subregion $\leftrightarrow$ Lake $\leftrightarrow$
Year so similarity operations execute directly against the indexed
database.

\section{Backend and Frontend}

The FastAPI backend provides services for lake metadata lookup,
analog similarity search, temporal feature retrieval, forecast
retrieval, report generation, and system status, separating cheap
data retrieval from heavy batch processing. The frontend, built on
React/Vite/TypeScript with Leaflet mapping, lets a user select a
lake, view its boundary, request analogs, visualize their locations
and scores, and inspect historical profiles alongside the forecast;
displayed geometry is drawn from the same PostGIS source used for
the analysis itself, avoiding disagreement between result and map.

\section{Asynchronous Processing and Progress Tracking}

Imagery download, raster merging, and model inference all take far
longer than an acceptable HTTP request, so each runs through
FastAPI's \code{BackgroundTasks} and returns immediately. A
distributed task queue was considered and not adopted: the durable
state it would provide already exists in the database, where every
row carries its own status. Progress is exposed through module-level
state guarded by a lock (counts, elapsed time, throughput, projected
completion), polled by the frontend; long jobs are also cancellable,
stopping after the current lake-year rather than mid-write.

\section{Server-Side B8 Acquisition}
\label{sec:b8}

Unlike \code{B2--B7}, which must be downloaded as pixels for Prithvi
inference, B8 is needed only as summary statistics, so the B8
pipeline performs the reduction entirely on Earth Engine's servers
using \code{Image.reduceRegions()}: lake polygons are batched
fifteen at a time and reduced against a single annual composite, so
only the resulting numbers are transferred and no imagery is
downloaded. A separate \code{b8\_status} column tracks this pipeline
independently, completed for all \textbf{8{,}227} lake-years.

\section{The Score Compression Problem}

Measured raw cosine scores across all five queries fall between
\textbf{0.9969} and \textbf{0.9987}, with the tenth-ranked analog
typically within \textbf{0.005} of the first --- expected since
every vector shares one encoder and describes the same general scene
type, so the absolute score is close to uninformative and only the
ordering carries signal. Ecological interpretation therefore
requires additional evidence from lake metadata, spatial maps, and
temporal indicators (Chapter~\ref{chap:ramsar}); a meaningful
absolute scale would require calibration against labelled ecosystem
pairs (Section~\ref{sec:future}).

\section{Temporal Profile Results}

The temporal layer provides a second, independent axis of
validation alongside spatial similarity: EcoTwin presents the full
ten-year series of all five indices, observation availability, and
the directional forecast with its contributing twins. Its value is
demonstrated concretely in Chapter~\ref{chap:ramsar}, where several
sites' top analogs are geographically unrelated to the query lake
yet still share its independently measured historical trend.

\section{Processing Reliability}

The system tolerates partial failure by design: a lake-year can be
pending, processing, completed, failed, or skipped because its
output already exists, making large multi-year jobs restartable
instead of requiring a complete restart after one failed request.

\section{Engineering Problems Encountered}

Several recurring problems shaped the implementation. Large Earth
Engine requests sometimes produced memory-limit errors, solved by
reducing export region size and moving processing locally; parallel
workers caused HTTP 429 and connection-pool errors, pushing the
pipeline toward smaller controlled worker counts and resumable jobs.
The early Sentinel-2 Level-1C fallback exposed different quality
bands than other collections, leading to collection-aware
preprocessing rather than a hard-coded cloud mask. Merged rasters
can become much larger than individual tiles, making compression and
tiled GeoTIFF writing important; the local filesystem once reached
100\% utilization, demonstrating that the pipeline must account for
intermediate storage, not only final dataset size. Finally, Earth
Engine authentication depends on external network services, so
authentication is kept separate from per-lake processing and
failures are recorded rather than silently producing incomplete
data.

Because the project was developed by multiple team members, a
database consolidation procedure was also designed: each developer's
PostgreSQL dump is restored into a temporary database, validated,
and merged into production, preserving PostGIS geometries and
pgvector embeddings in a way CSV-based migration could not.

The forecast is internally coherent: NDVI rises while NDWI falls by
a comparable magnitude every year (both share B8), and rising
red-edge slope and turbidity reinforce the same reading ---
\textbf{gradual vegetative encroachment and reduction of open
water} --- with the declining twin count (5, 4, 3) signalling 2028
should be read more cautiously than 2026.

\subsection{Documented Condition}

Chilika's weed cover exploded from $\sim$20 to $\sim$685
km\textsuperscript{2} between 1972 and 2000 as siltation reduced
salinity, prompting the 2000 sea-mouth reopening that sharply cut
weed cover.\footnote{Chilika Development Authority site history;
Down To Earth, ``Chilika: a lake in limbo'',
\url{https://www.downtoearth.org.in/environment/chilika-a-lake-in-limbo-30053}.}
Recent reporting describes renewed weed growth interfering with
fishing --- consistent with the gradual encroachment signal measured
here, a real but far milder trend than the pre-2000 crisis.

oscillating, no-sustained-trend signal measured here.

richness rather than a published condition trajectory.

\subsection{Interpretation}

Tso Morari is the only site where all five twins contribute to all
three forecast years ($n=5$ throughout), the best-supported forecast
in the study. The projection is mild --- modest NDVI/NDCI increases,
NDWI declining across all three years without reversal ---
appropriately undramatic for an ecosystem with no significant
vegetation dynamic.

\subsection{Documented Condition}

The literature on Tso Moriri attributes its water-level fluctuations
primarily to within-year forcing (snowmelt and glacial meltwater
against winter precipitation and evaporation) rather than a short
secular trend, while flagging accelerating glacier mass loss across
the wider Trans-Himalayan region as a longer-horizon
concern.\footnote{Extreme seasonal water-level changes in
high-altitude Himalayan lakes, \emph{Scientific Reports} (2023),
\url{https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-37667-z}.} The
near-decade stability measured here is consistent with a system
dominated by within-year forcing, but cannot speak to the slower
glacial retreat documented at the regional scale.

\subsection{Documented Condition}

Sasthamcotta's decline is extensively documented: elevated COD/BOD
and turbidity, shoreline encroachment removing an estimated 15\% of
the lake's perimeter, illegal sand extraction lowering water levels,
and over 40\% of native fish species disappeared in two
decades.\footnote{Water Quality Assessment of Sasthamcotta Lake,
Kollam, Kerala, \url{https://www.researchgate.net/publication/323628043}.}
This is a genuinely degrading system by every published account, but
the measured signal only \emph{partially} agrees: NDVI and red-edge
decline as expected, but NDWI \emph{rises} over the decade, the
opposite of what a shrinking lake would suggest. A plausible
reconciliation is that encroachment and sand mining chiefly erode
the lake's perimeter and depth --- under-represented by a
coverage-weighted mean over the remaining analysis cells --- while
the NDVI/NDCI decline more likely reflects falling phytoplankton
than the water-hyacinth pressure seen on other Kerala wetlands
(which would push NDVI up, not down). The measured signal and public
record should be read together here, each qualifying the other,
rather than one confirming the other.

\chapter{Discussion}
\label{chap:discussion}

\section{Strengths and Limitations}

The system's central strengths are scalable offline preprocessing
executed to completion with zero residual failures
(Chapter~\ref{chap:results}); trajectory-based retrieval that
matches ecological \emph{condition} rather than single-year
appearance, demonstrated by the Tso Morari--Pangong pairing
(Chapter~\ref{chap:ramsar}); and a decoder-free, fully auditable
forecasting layer that reports \emph{uncertain} rather than guessing
when evidence is thin (13.3\% abstention across 75 forecast cells).

Its limitations are structural. The ten-year window is not
uniformly complete, and cloud cover reduces valid Sentinel-2
observations. A high embedding similarity does not guarantee
ecological equivalence --- only the ranking is meaningful, not the
compressed absolute score --- and every result is conditional on the
fixed configuration of Table~\ref{tab:current_config}. A twin whose
matched window ends near 2025 has little observed future to
transfer, which thinned Beas's and Tawa/Sasthamcotta's later-horizon
forecasts to one or two twins (Chapter~\ref{chap:ramsar}) --- a
consequence of dataset extent, not a flaw in the classification
rule, which correctly reported \emph{uncertain} where warranted.
Finally, the documented-condition checks are corroborating evidence,
not calibrated validation, and their strength varies by site:
Chilika's signal lines up with independently reported weed growth,
Sasthamcotta's decline only partially agrees with the measured
signal, Beas and Tawa have no published trend to check against, and
Tso Morari's strongest corroboration is internal to the retrieval
method; formal hindcasting (Section~\ref{sec:hindcast_protocol}) is
the next step precisely because it does not require external data.

\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{chap:conclusion}

\section{Conclusion}

EcoTwin evolved from a conceptual ecosystem-comparison idea into a
complete, fully executed geospatial retrieval and forecasting
system: HydroLAKES and Sentinel-2 feed a buffered, tiled acquisition
pipeline into Prithvi embeddings, coverage-pooled to lake level and
indexed in pgvector, from which a five-year sliding-window
trajectory search ranks analogs and a decoder-free forecasting layer
transfers their measured index changes onto the target with a full
audit trail --- executed to completion over 829 lakes, ten years,
and zero outstanding failures.

Chapter~\ref{chap:ramsar} demonstrated the pipeline on five Ramsar
wetlands, returning a striking unsupervised validation (Tso Morari's
top analog is Pangong Tso, the only other high-altitude Ladakhi lake
in the cohort), a measured encroachment signal at Chilika consistent
with independently reported weed growth, a decade-long decline at
Sasthamcotta that only partially agrees with its own documented
degradation, and a forecasting method that correctly declined to
predict in 10 of 75 cells rather than manufacturing a confident
answer. The project's most significant contribution is the
integration of geospatial data engineering, foundation-model
inference, vector retrieval, temporal analysis, and visualization
into one reproducible workflow carried through to applied results.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future}

Priority items are executing the hindcasting protocol once further
years extend the dataset past its current single-cutoff limitation;
extending ingestion beyond 2025 to resolve \emph{uncertain} cells;
adding independent field-ecological validation; and migrating
trajectory search to pgvector's native ANN operators, since the
current brute-force scan will not remain interactive as the cohort
grows. Further directions include calibrating the similarity display
scale against labelled ecosystem pairs, learned-weighted pooling in
place of coverage-weighted mean, growing the cohort beyond 829
lakes, and deploying shared production infrastructure.

\section{Final Remarks}

EcoTwin's central idea is to use historically observed ecosystems as
computationally retrievable analogs rather than treating every
ecosystem as an isolated forecasting problem. The Ramsar case
studies show that this idea, carried through a fully processed
cohort, produces results that are specific, checkable against
independent knowledge where it exists, and honest about the limits
of what it can currently claim where the evidence ran out.
